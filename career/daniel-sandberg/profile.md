## Current role

Daniel Sandberg is the inaugural head coach of the FedEx University Couriers in Memphis, Tennessee.

He was appointed during 2025 so he could build FedEx University's inaugural program, staff and roster before the Couriers began playing in 2026. The early appointment made Sandberg more than the football coach. He became one of the principal architects of the entire program.

His responsibilities during the construction period include:

- defining the football identity
- recruiting the inaugural roster
- hiring the first coaching and support staffs
- helping design the football facilities and daily operation
- establishing academic and behavioral expectations
- building relationships with FedEx employees and the Memphis community
- selling recruits on a university with elite resources but no football history

FedEx University enters its first season in Tier 3 as an Independent.

## Playing career

Sandberg arrived in the United States from Sweden as an unconventional football prospect and eventually developed into an NFL running back.

Born in 1986, he followed the natural four-year college path and entered the 2008 NFL Draft at age 22.

He played eight seasons in the NFL:

1. two seasons with the Dallas Cowboys, 2008-2009
2. six seasons with the Green Bay Packers, 2010-2015

Sandberg was never treated as a generational superstar, but he became a highly respected professional runner known for vision, balance, reliability and an unusually detailed understanding of blocking structure.

He joined Green Bay in 2010 and immediately became part of the team that won Super Bowl XLV. During his six years with the Packers, he:

- won Super Bowl XLV in his first Green Bay season
- became the Packers' leading rusher for one season
- developed a reputation as a dependable pass protector and receiver
- became valued inside the organization for preparation and football intelligence
- played five seasons alongside linebacker AJ Hawk, from 2010 through 2014

Sandberg and Hawk were not merely members of the same organization. They shared the Super Bowl season and remained teammates for five years, giving Hawk direct knowledge of Sandberg's preparation, toughness, intelligence and locker-room personality.

Sandberg completed the 2015 season and announced his retirement during the 2016 offseason, around age 30. The decision was voluntary but driven by the accumulated physical cost of playing running back. Sandberg believed he could still contribute, but no longer felt he could prepare and recover at the standard he expected of himself over another full NFL season.

He finished his career with eight NFL seasons, one Super Bowl ring and the credibility of a player who had experienced roster insecurity, a championship run and a long tenure inside one of the league's most scrutinized organizations.

## Post-playing career

Sandberg did not move directly into full-time coaching after retirement.

His first years away from football included several overlapping roles:

- NFL and college football media work
- studio and game analysis for Scandinavian broadcasts
- occasional work with American sports networks
- commercial and corporate appearances
- leadership talks based on professional sports, preparation and team culture
- periods living and working in Sweden

The media work made him comfortable explaining football publicly without turning every discussion into empty television rhetoric. It also helped develop the communication style that later became central to his recruiting and program-building appeal.

## FedEx relationship

Sandberg's relationship with FedEx began during his post-playing media and commercial career.

FedEx used him in a series of European and transatlantic brand appearances built around preparation, reliability and performing under time pressure. He later participated in internal FedEx leadership events and employee programs in both the United States and Europe.

The relationship was not initially connected to football employment. Over several years, however, Sandberg became familiar to senior figures inside the company and developed a genuine understanding of FedEx culture, its Memphis identity and the scale of its employee network.

This mattered when FedEx University began searching for its founding head coach. Sandberg was not an outside celebrity introduced during the final interview process. He was already a trusted figure who understood the organization and could speak credibly to employees, executives, recruits and football staff.

## Coaching career

### Early coaching work

Sandberg entered coaching gradually.

He first worked with high-school players, initially as a volunteer and position specialist. His early focus was running backs, pass protection and offensive decision-making rather than simply teaching rushing technique.

During periods back in Sweden, he also assisted with player development and coaching education. That experience exposed him to programs with limited resources, mixed experience levels and players who could not be coached through assumption or tradition.

It shaped a central belief of his coaching philosophy:

> A coach should be able to explain why something works, not merely demand that players repeat it.

### College assistant

Sandberg returned to the United States for a full-time college position as a running backs coach. His NFL background opened the door, but his teaching ability and understanding of offensive structure allowed him to advance.

He later added responsibilities as run-game coordinator and recruiting contributor. He became known for connecting professional standards to college players without pretending that an NFL locker room and a college program were the same environment.

### Colorado State offensive coordinator

Sandberg's final position before FedEx University was offensive coordinator at Colorado State.

His Colorado State offenses were successful enough to establish him as a credible head-coaching candidate without making him the obvious choice for every major vacancy. The Rams became more aggressive, more flexible and more difficult to prepare for under his direction.

Although Sandberg came from a running-back background, his offense was not built around protecting his own playing legacy. He adopted an Air Raid foundation, then incorporated:

- a serious and varied rushing attack
- running backs as receivers
- motion and formation changes
- quarterback freedom at the line of scrimmage
- aggressive fourth-down decision-making
- tempo used selectively rather than constantly

Colorado State's improvement demonstrated that Sandberg could design an offense, manage a staff and recruit to a clear vision. It did not answer whether he could run an entire program, which made the FedEx appointment both ambitious and defensible.

## Why FedEx chose Sandberg

FedEx University did not hire Sandberg solely because he had played in the NFL.

The search committee believed he offered a rare combination:

### Professional credibility

He had played eight NFL seasons, led the Packers in rushing for one season and won Super Bowl XLV. Recruits and families would immediately recognize that he understood the standard required at the highest level.

### Proven coaching development

He had deliberately worked through position coaching, player development, recruiting and offensive coordination rather than assuming his playing career entitled him to a head job.

### Existing FedEx trust

His previous corporate and employee work meant he already understood the organization and had relationships inside it.

### International perspective

As a Swede who built a career in American football, Sandberg understood what it meant to enter an established system without inherited connections. FedEx believed that perspective suited a university attempting to enter college football without inherited tradition.

### Founder mentality

Most candidates approached the vacancy as a football job. Sandberg presented it as the construction of an institution.

His proposal covered culture, recruiting, staffing, employee engagement, player education and the identity of a team representing both Memphis and a global company.

### Willingness to accept the full project

FedEx did not need a coach interested only in Saturdays. It needed someone willing to recruit players to a team that did not yet exist, hire every member of staff and help turn corporate resources into a credible football environment.

Sandberg wanted precisely that challenge.

## Coaching philosophy

Sandberg's program is intended to be demanding without becoming performatively harsh.

His core principles are:

- explain the purpose behind the work
- treat players as developing adults
- prepare with professional detail
- communicate honestly about roles and performance
- build an offense around available talent rather than personal vanity
- use aggression deliberately
- recruit intelligence, competitiveness and adaptability
- avoid confusing tradition with correctness

He wants FedEx to play modern, aggressive football while remaining structurally sound.

His preferred offensive identity is an adaptable Air Raid. The passing game creates space and forces defenses to reveal themselves, while the running game punishes light boxes and protects the offense from becoming one-dimensional.

## Public reputation at appointment

Sandberg's hiring generated both fascination and skepticism.

Supporters saw:

- a Super Bowl-winning former player
- an eight-year NFL veteran with six seasons in Green Bay
- a rising offensive coach
- an intelligent communicator
- a natural representative for an unconventional university

Critics saw:

- a first-time head coach
- limited top-level coaching experience
- a corporate relationship that may have influenced the search
- a university using NFL celebrity to manufacture immediate legitimacy

Both interpretations contain some truth.

Sandberg was hired partly because of his résumé and partly because FedEx already trusted him. The appointment will ultimately be judged by whether he can turn extraordinary resources and a compelling idea into an actual football program.

## Initial coaching challenge

FedEx University has elite facilities, staffing resources, recruiting infrastructure and financial backing. It has no returning roster, inherited culture, alumni network or established football tradition.

The inaugural roster must be assembled through:

- high-school recruiting
- junior-college recruiting
- the transfer portal

The intended roster identity is talented but incomplete. FedEx may attract several players above the normal level of a new program, but the offensive line, defensive rotation and overall depth will require time.

Promotion from Tier 3 is the primary competitive objective for the inaugural season. In the current structure, approximately four wins may be enough, depending on the national results and placement rules.
