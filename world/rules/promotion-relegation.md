# Promotion and Relegation Rules

## League structure

| Tier | Role | Conferences or status | Teams |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Tier 1 | Elite level | Big 12 and Big Ten | 32 |
| Tier 2 | Promotion level | Eight conferences | 96 |
| Tier 3 | Sit-out and independent tier | Independents | 6 |
| Total | | | 134 |

Core principle:

> Team performance decides tier placement. Geography decides conference placement.

## Tier 1

- Two conferences: Big 12 and Big Ten
- 16 teams per conference
- Two divisions of eight
- Nine intended conference games
- Conference championship between division winners
- Two protected rivals where possible

### Geographic allocation

- Los Angeles acts as the western anchor for the Big 12.
- New York City acts as the eastern anchor for the Big Ten.
- Assign the 32 qualified teams by geographic proximity.
- Resolve border cases until both conferences contain 16 teams.
- Within each conference, the eight northernmost teams form the North division and the eight southernmost form the South division.

## Tier 2

Eight conferences of 12 teams:

- Pac-12
- Mountain West
- Conference USA
- AAC
- Sun Belt
- SEC
- MAC
- ACC

Rules:

- No divisions
- Nine intended conference games
- Championship game between the top two teams
- Conference champion earns promotion

### Macro-regions

| Macro-region | Identity | Conferences |
|---|---|---|
| West | Pacific, Mountain, Desert West | Pac-12 and Mountain West |
| Central | Texas, Plains, lower Midwest | Conference USA and AAC |
| South | Gulf, Deep South, Florida, Carolinas | Sun Belt and SEC |
| East/North | Great Lakes, Appalachia, Atlantic, Northeast | MAC and ACC |

Split the 96 Tier 2 teams into four geographic macro-regions of 24, then divide each region into two conferences of 12.

## Tier 3

Tier 3 contains six Independents.

The current dynasty uses earned return from Tier 3 rather than automatic annual return. Record the exact movement rule applied in each season archive when determining which Independents return and which Tier 2 teams fall.

## Annual movement

### Tier 1 to Tier 2

- Relegate the bottom four Big 12 teams.
- Relegate the bottom four Big Ten teams.
- Total relegated: eight.

Use the game's standings order and conference results as the primary basis when overall records conflict.

### Tier 2 to Tier 1

- Promote each of the eight Tier 2 conference champions.
- Total promoted: eight.

### Tier 2 and Tier 3

- Identify the lowest-performing Tier 2 teams according to the active season rule.
- Determine which Independents earned promotion.
- Preserve any team that remains in Tier 3 in the season archive.
- Document tiebreak decisions explicitly.

## Playoff logic

The game uses a fixed 12-team College Football Playoff.

Working behavior:

- Five highest-ranked conference champions receive automatic access.
- Seven at-large teams complete the field.
- Tier 2 champions may earn both promotion and playoff access.
- Tier 1 champions are not assumed to be guaranteed playoff places if the game ranks them too low.

## Offseason workflow

1. Record final Tier 1 standings.
2. Record all Tier 2 championship winners.
3. Record Tier 3 results and the worst Tier 2 teams.
4. Record the playoff and final rankings.
5. Apply all promotions and relegations.
6. Rebuild Tier 1 geographically at 16 teams per conference.
7. Rebuild Tier 2 by macro-region at 12 teams per conference.
8. Publish the next-season alignment.
9. Verify conference count, divisions, championship settings, nine conference games, protected rivals, and venues before advancing.

## Manual decisions

Manual judgment is allowed only when:

- a conference must reach exactly 12 or 16 teams
- a team lies on a natural geographic border
- the game's conference tools require a practical adjustment
- records or standings require a documented tiebreak

Manual judgment must never be used to protect a strong or prestigious team.
