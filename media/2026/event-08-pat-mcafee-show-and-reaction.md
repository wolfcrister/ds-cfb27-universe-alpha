# Event 08: Pat McAfee Show Appearance and Reaction

## Context

Following National Signing Day, the completion of FedEx University's 60-player inaugural scholarship class and the first full-team practice, head coach Daniel Sandberg appeared on The Pat McAfee Show. The appearance followed a local Memphis radio interview in which Sandberg confirmed that promotion is already the expectation for the inaugural 2026 season.

AJ Hawk participated in the segment as Sandberg's former Green Bay Packers teammate.

## Core football points confirmed

- Graham Harrell and Daniel Sandberg build the weekly offensive game plan together.
- Harrell calls the plays on game day.
- Sandberg retains the authority to override individual calls when he chooses.
- Sandberg describes the relationship with Harrell as collaborative, based on trust, friendship and different skill sets.
- The program deliberately avoids surrounding the head coach with sycophants. Sandberg wants trusted staff members who are willing to challenge him.
- Sandberg acknowledges that building an FBS football program from scratch is essentially an experiment because it has not been done this way at this level.
- Promotion remains the explicit first-year goal, but the program will still allow itself time to develop.
- Sandberg's desired first-game impression is that FedEx University is ready and looks like a serious team for the years ahead.

## Tight end discussion

Sandberg doubled down on his positive assessment of the tight end room and jokingly described modern elite tight ends as "giant psychos who do everything."

He contrasted the modern position with earlier eras, noting that elite contemporary tight ends such as Rob Gronkowski, Travis Kelce, George Kittle and Tucker Kraft combine receiving, blocking, size, strength and versatility.

The phrase quickly became the most viral football quote from the interview.

## Sandberg and Harrell dynamic

When asked how disagreements with Harrell would work, Sandberg joked that if the disagreement concerns the running game, he can simply show Harrell his 2014 NFL rushing-title plaque.

Sandberg then clarified that the relationship is a genuine give-and-take partnership built around different experiences and complementary skills.

Asked what would happen if Sandberg overrode a Harrell call and the replacement failed, Sandberg joked that he would "smack him around" because he was a running back and Harrell was a quarterback. The comment was clearly delivered while laughing and was treated as locker-room humor by the hosts.

AJ Hawk joked that Harrell should be fine because quarterbacks spend their careers avoiding contact.

## Roster and early-season expectations

Asked how quickly FedEx would change course after a hypothetical 1-2 start, Sandberg first joked:

> Then we'll just buy a new team. We can probably have it shipped overnight.

He then explained that the entire project is new to everyone involved. While promotion is the stated goal, the program is not treating a slow start as proof that the concept has failed.

The staff believes strongly in the foundation but recognizes that sixty newly assembled players need time to become a functioning football team.

## Closing answer

When McAfee asked what Sandberg wants viewers to think after watching FedEx University's first game, Sandberg answered:

> That we're ready. That this looks like a serious team in the years to come.

This became the most serious and widely repeated line from the appearance.

# Reaction cycle

## National headlines

### ESPN

**Sandberg: Promotion is the expectation for inaugural FedEx University team**

Coverage focuses on the combination of immediate ambition and the acknowledgement that the team is still developing.

### The Athletic

**FedEx University is still an experiment. Daniel Sandberg is treating it like a football program.**

The analysis emphasizes the conventional football hierarchy beneath the unusual institution: collaborative game planning, Harrell calling plays, head-coach veto authority and a performance-based depth chart.

### Sports Illustrated

**From "giant psychos" to promotion talk, Sandberg turns another interview into appointment viewing**

The tight end quote becomes the centerpiece.

### CBS Sports

**FedEx coach says team can develop while still expecting immediate promotion**

The central debate becomes whether those two ideas are compatible.

## Pat McAfee social clips

Three clips drive the show's post-interview traffic:

1. **"GIANT PSYCHOS WHO DO EVERYTHING"** - Sandberg's modern tight-end description.
2. **"Then we'll just buy a new team. We can probably have it shipped overnight."** - the joke about reacting to a poor start.
3. **The 2014 rushing-title plaque joke** - Sandberg's response to hypothetical run-game disagreements with Harrell.

AJ Hawk reposts the rushing-title clip with the comment that Sandberg would absolutely do it.

## Football-analysis reaction

Analysts note that beneath the humor, Sandberg gave unusually clear answers about structure and decision-making.

The main takeaways are:

- the offensive hierarchy is conventional and defined;
- Harrell has meaningful autonomy as play caller;
- Sandberg retains final authority;
- Miles Kennedy is clearly leading the quarterback competition even without an official depth chart;
- the early depth chart is expected to remain fluid;
- the tight end group is emerging as a genuine personnel strength rather than merely a joke.

## Memphis reaction

Local coverage focuses on Sandberg's willingness to say promotion is the expectation while still prioritizing experience and long-term development.

Local radio summarizes the appearance as a combination of team-building philosophy, mutual respect, jokes about workplace violence and unusual enthusiasm for tight ends.

A recurring debate asks whether four wins and promotion should be considered sufficient given FedEx University's resources.

## r/CFB reaction

Two threads dominate:

### Tight ends

**FedEx HC Daniel Sandberg on modern tight ends: "They're all giant psychos who do everything."**

Top reactions treat the quote as both ridiculous and strangely accurate.

### Overnight roster joke

**FedEx HC jokes they'll "buy a new team and ship it overnight" if the season starts badly**

The thread immediately fills with FedEx logistics and transfer-portal jokes.

## Former-player reaction

Former Packers players joke that threatening Harrell with the 2014 rushing-title plaque is entirely believable.

AJ Hawk later provides the more serious counterweight:

> People see Daniel joking around and think that is the whole personality. It isn't. He is extremely competitive. The jokes stop very quickly when football starts.

This becomes an important part of Sandberg's emerging national image.

## Tight end culture subplot

The "giant psychos" phrase spreads beyond FedEx coverage and is adopted jokingly by football fans as a modern tight-end description.

FedEx University's social account responds:

> Official institutional terminology has not changed.
>
> Tight Ends remain listed as Tight Ends.

A follow-up adds only:

> Internally, however...

## Emerging national perception

Before the appearance, FedEx University was widely framed as a corporate novelty with unlimited resources.

Afterward, the emerging view becomes:

> A corporate novelty with unlimited resources, a coherent football structure, a charismatic head coach and enough talent that the joke may stop being funny for opponents.

The dominant preseason question shifts toward:

**How quickly does FedEx University stop being a novelty?**
