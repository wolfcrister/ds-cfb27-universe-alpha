# First Practice Press Conference

## Context

FedEx University held the first public practice in program history after completing its inaugural 60-player recruiting class.

The practice represented the first time the full roster worked together on the field. Media attention focused on roster readiness, offensive identity, the unexpectedly strong tight end group and the question of how leadership would emerge inside a team with no inherited veterans or established hierarchy.

## Press conference transcript

**Reporter:** Coach, sixty players have now signed, the roster finally exists, and expectations changed almost overnight. When you looked at the completed group for the first time, did you see a team ready to compete, or simply sixty individual players who now need to become one?

**Daniel Sandberg:** It's the first practice guys, of course there's lots of work to do before this is a well oiled machine. But I think the parts are mostly here. We're more than happy with the recruitment.

**Reporter:** You've said the parts are mostly here. What part of the roster surprised you most once everybody was finally on the same field together?

**Sandberg:** I have to say our TE group looks more solid than expected, so that's fun.

**Reporter:** Is that a group you expect to feature heavily in the offense, then? With Graham Harrell's Air Raid background, people probably assumed FedEx would live in four-receiver sets. Are we actually going to see more tight ends than expected?

**Sandberg:** We're not married to a formation or personnel, but we have a concept and an idea.

**Reporter:** When you say "a concept and an idea," what does that actually mean on the field? What are the non-negotiables of a Daniel Sandberg offense, regardless of whether you line up with one tight end, two tight ends or four receivers?

**Sandberg:** The offense is built from the Oline and out. Thats number one. Strong oline. Then we want to pass the ball a lot, to open up the field.

**Reporter:** So the pass is not the identity by itself. The identity is controlling the game up front, then using the passing game to stretch the defense until the run and everything underneath becomes easier?

And with this tight end group looking better than expected, does that give you more ways to create those matchups without changing the core idea?

**Sandberg:** Probably yeah. Tight enda today usually are big, strong monsters that also can catch.

**Reporter:** That sounds like you see them less as a traditional position group and more as matchup tools.

Big enough to punish smaller defensive backs, athletic enough to stress linebackers, and useful enough as blockers that the defense cannot immediately diagnose the play from the personnel.

Is that the appeal for you?

**Sandberg:** Couldn't have said it better really.

**Reporter:** Last one from me, Coach. You have sixty new players, no inherited culture and no established veterans to enforce standards for you. Who sets the tone in that locker room right now? Is it the staff, the older transfers, or are you waiting to see which players naturally take ownership?

**Sandberg:** The natural leaders will show up soon enough. It's usually quite easy to see who the guys follow.

**Reporter:** Does that mean you avoid appointing captains too early? Let the hierarchy form naturally first, then formalize it once the team has shown you who carries real influence?

**Sandberg:** I think it's necessary to do it that way. It's a completely new group after all.

## Canon established

- FedEx University has begun full-team practice with the completed inaugural roster.
- Sandberg considers the roster's core components largely present, while acknowledging that the group is far from a finished team.
- The tight end group appears stronger than the staff expected.
- The offense is not committed to one formation or personnel grouping.
- The offensive foundation begins with a strong offensive line.
- FedEx intends to pass frequently in order to widen and stress the field.
- Tight ends are viewed as flexible matchup pieces who can block, catch and create personnel conflicts.
- Captains will not be appointed prematurely.
- Leadership will be allowed to emerge naturally from the completely new roster.

## Emerging storylines

- Which tight ends become meaningful parts of the inaugural offense?
- How quickly can the offensive line become the foundation Sandberg wants?
- Who becomes the first captain in FedEx University history?
- Which players naturally command the confidence of the new locker room?
