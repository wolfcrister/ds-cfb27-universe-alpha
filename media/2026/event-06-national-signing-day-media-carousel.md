# National Signing Day 2026: The 60-Player Inaugural Class

## Event status

Confirmed canon.

FedEx University completed construction of its inaugural roster by signing 60 scholarship players on National Signing Day.

The class is built primarily around three-star prospects, with several four-star recruits and a smaller number of developmental two-star players. It combines high-school recruits, junior-college additions and transfers.

All 60 players receive full athletic scholarships.

## Official announcement

### FedEx University signs 60-player inaugural class

FedEx University signed the first football recruiting class in school history, adding 60 scholarship players from the high-school, junior-college and transfer ranks.

The class gives head coach Daniel Sandberg and offensive coordinator Graham Harrell the foundation of the roster that will represent FedEx University during its inaugural 2026 season.

> "This is not a normal recruiting class, because this is not a normal situation. We did not need to improve an existing roster. We needed to create one."
>
> "Sixty young men have chosen to become the first players in the history of this university. Whatever happens from here, nobody can ever take that away from them."
>
> Daniel Sandberg

The group is headlined by Memphis quarterback and Georgia transfer Miles Kennedy, four-star Whitehaven cornerback Jalen Rucker, junior-college defensive tackle DeShawn Booker and first commitment Marcus Whitfield.

Sandberg described the class as deliberately broad.

> "We have players who could have gone almost anywhere. We have players who were overlooked. We have transfers who need a real opportunity, junior-college players who had to take a longer road and freshmen who may need two or three years before they are ready."
>
> "They are all FedEx University football players now."

## National media coverage

### ESPN

**FedEx University signs 60 players in first recruiting class**

The national story focuses on the scale of the undertaking. ESPN describes it as less like a normal recruiting class and more like an expansion draft conducted through high schools, junior colleges and the transfer portal.

The central question becomes whether 60 individual recruiting decisions can become one functioning locker room before the first kickoff.

### The Athletic

**Inside the 60-player class that created FedEx football overnight**

The class is judged unusually strong for a new Tier 3 program:

- better top-end talent than expected
- strong regional recruiting
- useful immediate help from transfers and JUCO players
- legitimate depth concerns despite the class size
- almost no inherited leadership structure

The summary assessment:

> FedEx did not purchase a championship roster. It may have purchased enough competence to avoid looking like an expansion team.

### The Commercial Appeal

**Sixty signatures, one question: Can FedEx University turn Memphis talent into Memphis loyalty?**

Local coverage focuses on the number of players from Memphis and surrounding communities, especially four-star cornerback Jalen Rucker.

The class is treated as a direct challenge to Memphis and several regional programs that traditionally recruit the same three-star tier.

### Daily Memphian

**FedEx awards full scholarships to entire inaugural football class**

The financial model becomes a major part of the story. FedEx confirms that every player receives full athletic support, academic assistance and access to the university's corporate career network.

### Sports Illustrated

**Miles Kennedy bets his career on a football program that has never played**

Kennedy becomes the national face of signing day.

An anonymous rival assistant says:

> "He could have found playing time at a lot of real programs."

Kennedy responds:

> "Guess we'll find out how real it is."

### FOX Sports

**Daniel Sandberg's first roster is built around opportunity, not patience**

The class is presented as a collection of players attracted by immediate opportunity:

- blocked transfers
- overlooked regional prospects
- JUCO players seeking major roles
- three-star recruits unwilling to wait behind deeper rosters
- local players attracted by becoming program founders

## Recruiting evaluation

Recruiting services struggle to compare a 60-player startup class with normal national classes.

Representative estimates place FedEx:

- around No. 28 nationally in total incoming talent
- around No. 41 when class size is normalized
- No. 1 among Tier 3 programs
- the most unusual class in modern recruiting history

The high-school distribution is centered on three-star recruits, supplemented by several four-stars and a few two-star developmental players.

## Check the Mic

The roster is considered too talented to dismiss as a normal startup.

### Miles Kennedy

The most important variable on the roster. He has mobility, arm talent and SEC development, but little meaningful game experience.

> "He is a veteran in age and preparation, but almost a freshman in actual quarterback reps."

### DeShawn Booker

The safest immediate projection. An approximately 84-overall JUCO defensive tackle should be one of the strongest players on much of the Tier 3 schedule.

> "He is going to ruin at least one opponent's entire afternoon."

### Jalen Rucker

The best long-term prospect, with Tier 1 physical traits but unfinished technique.

### Marcus Whitfield

A symbolic and developmental signing who may be forced into action earlier than ideal.

Overall assessment:

> FedEx will look more talented than organized early. By November, that could reverse.

## Reaction inside college football

Regional programs are irritated because FedEx did not primarily take their five-star targets. It absorbed a significant portion of the regional three-star tier.

A recruiting coordinator summarizes the disruption:

> "They did not steal our five-stars. They took our entire second tier."

Major SEC programs remain mostly unconcerned by the high-school class, but Kennedy's transfer demonstrates that FedEx can offer a credible destination to experienced players blocked at elite schools.

Tier 3 opponents are much more alarmed. FedEx appears likely to have:

- one of the best quarterbacks in the tier
- one of its best defensive tackles
- several Power-level athletes
- unusually strong facilities and depth for a new entrant

## Final narrative shift

The inaugural class does not make FedEx University a finished team. It makes the program dangerous.

The roster still has no shared experience, no established captains and almost no veteran continuity. Many players were recruited on the promise of immediate opportunity, creating obvious future competition and transfer risks.

However, the national question has changed.

It is no longer:

> Can FedEx field a credible team?

It is now:

> How quickly should FedEx be expected to win?
